# Madras Magic Restaurant — Client Design Profile

Source: Design Iteration Tracker, ~60 logged rows, late March to late June 2026.
Client: Madras Magic Restaurant, an Indian restaurant in Taiwan (Instagram: @madrasmagic_indiancuisine).
Designer on record: Manikandan. This profile exists to help Vengatesh anticipate this client's
preferences before iteration rounds happen, not after.

This is a living document. When new iteration outcomes come in, add evidence to the relevant
section below rather than just restating a generic rule.

---

## 1. Hard rules (near-zero tolerance, repeat offenders)

### 1.1 Text accuracy — English AND Chinese
This is the single most frequent rejection cause in the entire history.
- Repeated Chinese spelling corrections across multiple separate projects (Mother's Day poster,
  Tuesday/Friday posts, stamp card).
- Wrong year caught on a cricket banner (2025 instead of 2026) — date/year fields need a deliberate
  double-check pass, not just a glance. **Confirmed directly from source files**: two near-identical
  versions of the Madras Magic Premier League cricket banner were rejected solely because the
  tournament date read "June 6-7,13-14 2025"; the approved version is visually identical in every
  other respect (same dense logo composition — Indian flag, trophy, Taipei 101 line-art, multiple
  cricket club logos, Yingfeng Cricket Ground) with only the year corrected to 2026. This is the
  cleanest evidence in the whole profile that a single wrong digit can cause two full rejection
  rounds on an otherwise-approved design — proofread dates with extra care, especially on dense,
  logo-heavy event banners where a small date badge is easy to overlook.
- Missing restaurant name was flagged as a rejection reason on its own (stamp card, v1).
- **Action**: Treat all on-design text (English and Chinese) as requiring a dedicated proofreading
  pass separate from the visual design pass. For Chinese text specifically, don't rely on a single
  machine translation — verify with a native speaker or back-translate before sending the first draft.

### 1.2 No "AI-looking" food or scenes, especially for print
- A meal combo banner was rejected explicitly for "AI dominant look in food and not suitable for
  printing."
- **Action**: Food imagery should look like real photography or photo-grounded edits. Avoid
  AI-generated food renders that have the telltale overly smooth/plastic/uncanny look — this is
  a named, explicit dealbreaker, not a vague aesthetic preference.

### 1.3 Print-readiness is non-negotiable for printed material
- A lunch meal box banner was rejected for "colour not suitable for printing" — twice in the same
  project before correction.
- A poster was approved in design but then the *delivered file size/dimensions* were wrong,
  requiring a re-send even after visual approval.
- A PDF poster had loading issues for the client, requiring re-delivery in both JPEG and PDF.
- **Action**: For anything described as going to print (posters, banners, resin art, stamp cards),
  proof colors assuming print output, not just screen brightness. Always confirm final exported
  file dimensions match the brief's stated size exactly. Default to delivering both JPEG and PDF
  unless told otherwise.

### 1.4 Exact physical/pixel dimensions are taken literally
Specific sizes seen across briefs: A3, 6'×10' (cricket banner), 64×110 (combo poster), 180×80
(restaurant identity banner), 2-inch (resin art). These are stated as hard specs by the client,
not approximate guidance.
- **Action**: Confirm the requested size before starting, and verify the final export matches it
  before sending.

## 2. Strong preferences (recurring but not absolute)

### 2.1 Color must serve legibility and mood, not just decoration
- Earliest logged rejection ever: "first pic got rejected due to over colourful" (April menu poster).
- Later rejections trend toward the *opposite* problem: backgrounds too light/washed out causing
  missing content, or background and food color too similar (low contrast), or background needing
  to be darker/black behind a logo.
- **Reading**: this isn't "use fewer colors" or "use more colors" — it's that color choices must
  preserve contrast and legibility of text/logo/food, and must look intentional rather than either
  garish or washed-out. Check contrast between background and both food and text before sending.

### 2.2 Tamil identity + Taiwan local context, blended carefully
- A restaurant identity banner explicitly combined a Thiruvalluvar statue, Taj Mahal, and known
  Taiwan buildings — design iterated to get the balance and lighting right (buildings "too dark,"
  needed to be "lighter").
- A separate banner added a Thirukural (Tamil couplet) placed at the top of the Thiruvalluvar statue.
- A cricket promotion banner replaced a generic ball/tree motif with "known buildings of Thailand/Taiwan"
  per client request — client prefers recognizable real-world local landmarks over generic stock motifs.
- A near-miss: a restaurant identity poster was rejected because an arch design made it "look in
  muslim theme," which was not the intent — client wanted highlighted restaurant-name fonts instead.
  **This signals the client is alert to unintended cultural/religious signaling in architectural motifs
  and wants the Tamil/Indian identity expressed clearly and unambiguously (e.g. via statue, script,
  Thirukural) rather than through generic "Indian-coded" architecture that can misread as something else.**
- **Action**: When a design needs to express "Indian-ness," lean on explicit, specific cultural
  markers (Thiruvalluvar, Thirukural, Tamil script, specific landmarks) rather than generic
  decorative motifs (arches, domes) that could be misread. Pair with real, recognizable Taiwan
  landmarks rather than generic local-flavor imagery.

### 2.3 Bilingual content (English + Chinese) is standard, not optional
Chinese text appears as a requirement across multiple unrelated projects (posters, stamp card,
weekday reel captions). Treat Chinese-language text as a default requirement for customer-facing
material in Taiwan, not an extra.

### 2.4 Reels/video: real motion, clarity, and audio over polish
- First long-running video project (started 9-Apr) needed 11 iteration rounds. Key feedback points:
  - needed audio added, and animation/speed increased ("little fast")
  - rejected twice for plain "video clarity" issues
  - rejected for background issue and text color issue separately
  - eventually required *raw footage* from the client because edited versions werenren't sharp enough
  - final ask before approval: "add more food photos"
- A separate reel: client asked to increase food animation/movement and add audio before approving.
- A weekday-special reel: approved only after swapping a specific dish shown for a different one
  on request (dosa → ghee dosa) — specificity about which dish represents which day matters.
- **Action**: For reels, prioritize: (1) source footage sharpness/clarity over editing tricks —
  ask for raw footage early if provided clips look soft, (2) background music, (3) faster pacing
  with visible food movement, (4) enough actual food shots, not just branding/text screens.

### 2.5 Reference images are meant to be followed closely on v1
Many briefs include "with reference pic" / "same content as reference image" / "following the
previous banner style." First-draft rejections often happen specifically *because* v1 deviated from
the reference (wrong content, wrong style) rather than for creative reasons.
- **Action**: When a reference image is provided, treat v1 as a fidelity pass to that reference.
  Save creative deviation/improvement suggestions for a second round, framed as an explicit option
  ("here's per the reference, and here's a variation if you want to consider it") rather than
  substituting your own interpretation on the first try.

### 2.6 Format/delivery details the client cares about
- Footer elements: at least once a footer was explicitly asked to be removed (menu poster).
- Logo background color: asked to change to black at least once; logo text color also corrected
  to match brand/logo color rather than plain white.
- File formats: JPEG + PDF combination preferred for print material when PDF alone caused issues.

## 2.7 Visual brand system (confirmed from 10 actual approved designs)

Unlike the sections above (inferred from rejection *text*), this section is grounded directly in
final, client-approved artwork: logo/profile assets, a mocktail post, a loyalty/stamp card, a
cricket tournament banner, and four food-hero posts (Cheera Paneer, Idli vs Dosa, Tandoori Chicken
Briyani, Cheese Crispy Sticks). Treat this as the most reliable evidence in this whole profile,
since these are outputs the client actually accepted, not just feedback about rejected drafts.

**Logo lockup — reused identically, do not reinvent it per project:**
- Circular badge: gold "M" monogram with a wheat/grain motif, on a black circle, ringed by Tamil
  text ("உணவு அளித்து மகிழ்") and "ELITE TASTE OF SOUTH INDIA" in gold.
- Standard header block (seen on nearly every food post): black rounded-rectangle bar containing
  [logo badge] + Chinese name "馬德拉斯魔法印度料理" (large, gold) + "MADRAS MAGIC INDIAN CUISINE" (gold,
  small caps, serif-ish display font) + "Elite Taste of South India" (orange/gold script font,
  italic, smallest).
- This exact header block is a reusable template — when building any new post, drop this header in
  rather than redesigning branding from scratch each time.
- **Second confirmed variant (added 02-Jul-2026, from the Pani Puri post)**: a compact version of
  the same header, scaled down into a top-left-only badge rather than a full-width bar. Used when
  bold display type needs to dominate the top of the frame. See `design_generation_guide.md` for
  when to pick which variant.

**Color palette is mood-coded by content type, not arbitrary:**
- **Deep maroon/burgundy + gold**: heritage, identity, loyalty/stamp card, cultural content (Taj
  Mahal imagery, Indian motifs). Conveys premium/traditional.
- **Dark forest green + gold**: hero food shots for "exclusive"/premium menu items (biryani, idli/dosa
  comparison). Conveys upscale, appetite-forward.
- **Warm cream/beige + photographic food**: lighter promotional posts (cheese crispy sticks). Conveys
  clean, appetizing, less "loud."
- **Bold poster-type + bright photo**: trendy/seasonal/limited items (blue mocktail post uses huge
  blue typographic treatment with a real product photo breaking through the letters).
- **Reading**: color choice follows the *category* of content (heritage vs. premium dish vs. casual
  promo vs. trendy/seasonal) — when starting a new brief, identify which of these four buckets it
  fits before picking a palette, rather than picking colors from instinct alone.

**Food photography is consistently real/photographic, plated and styled** — never illustrative or
AI-rendered. Garnishes (edible flowers, mint, chili threads), styled plates, props (wooden boards,
brass bowls, banana leaf textures) appear repeatedly. This directly confirms the "no AI-look" hard
rule from rejection history — the approved bar is genuine food photography or photo-real editing.

**Bilingual text hierarchy is consistent:**
- Chinese headline/title: large, bold, dominant — usually the biggest text on the page.
- English name/translation: secondary, smaller, often in a distinct typeface (elegant serif or
  display caps) directly below or beside the Chinese.
- Small captions/badges (e.g. "台北限定 / TAIPEI EXCLUSIVE") appear as a secondary bilingual pairing,
  same pattern at smaller scale.
- **Action**: When given English-only or Chinese-only copy, default to producing both, in this
  size/hierarchy relationship, rather than treating one language as an afterthought caption.

**Taiwan + India identity blending, confirmed visually:**
- A circular badge design pairs Taipei 101 (line-art) directly with a Thiruvalluvar statue
  (line-art, holding a scroll/Thirukural reference) — side by side, same line weight, same teal
  background. This is the clearest possible confirmation of the "explicit, paired local landmarks"
  pattern noted in section 2.2 — it's not occasional, it's a designed motif.
- The loyalty/stamp card uses Taj Mahal + ornate Indian textile/curtain motifs + gift boxes with a
  warm, inviting, slightly fantastical "Indian palace" aesthetic (soft-focus background, rich gold
  text on dark red) for the India side, paired with Chinese reward-tier text.
- The cricket tournament banner pairs the Indian flag, a cricket trophy, and Taipei 101 line-art
  in-frame together, plus multiple real cricket club logos (Taipei Indians, Taipei Stallions, etc.)
  — confirms client is comfortable with dense, logo-heavy compositions for community/event content,
  as long as everything is crisp, correctly attributed, and legible (no logo collisions or overlap).

**Typography notes:**
- Display headlines (e.g. "CHEERA PANEER," "MOCK TAIL") use bold, tightly-set, sometimes overlapping
  condensed sans/slab type as a graphic background element — large enough to be a design element in
  its own right, with the dish photo placed on top/over it.
- Script/cursive accent font (seen in "Elite Taste of South India" and "Mocktail" specials) is used
  sparingly as a secondary accent, never for primary information.

## 2.8 Live feed patterns (confirmed from 18 scrolling screenshots of the actual Instagram feed)

This batch is different from section 2.7's evidence: these are live screenshots of the actual
published feed (with dates, captions, hashtags, like counts, and several reels/videos), not just
isolated design files. This is the richest evidence available — it shows the full publishing
pattern, not just individual artwork.

**The "calm plate vs. dynamic splash" diptych is a recurring signature template, not a one-off.**
Both Cheera Paneer (Apr 7/10) and Golden Ghee Masala Chicken (Apr 15) use the identical structure:
left half = clean plated shot on a dark background; right half = the same dish "exploding" with
sauce/ingredients flying mid-air, separated by a thin vertical line. This is a reusable post format
— when a new dish needs a hero post and no other format is specified, this diptych is a safe,
proven default to propose.

**Caption and hashtag formula is fixed, not improvised per post.** Nearly every food post repeats
the same hashtag block: `#台灣美食 #台北美食 #忠孝復興美食 #南京復興美食 #印度咖哩 #印度料理` (plus a couple more,
varying slightly). Captions are bilingual, written in a warm/poetic tone in Chinese (e.g. "嫩滑雞肉慢火燉煮，
融合濃郁番茄與奶油醬汁" for butter chicken) with the English dish name as a secondary line, not a literal
translation of the full Chinese copy.
- **Action**: When drafting captions for new posts, reuse this exact (or near-identical) hashtag
  block rather than generating new tags, and write Chinese copy first as evocative/poetic
  description, with English playing a supporting/title role rather than full translation.

**Reels confirmed — pattern now visible (previously only inferred from rejection text):**
- Dark, moody background (deep teal/black) with steam or smoke effects rising from the dish.
- Slow text reveal animation, Chinese text dominant, appearing progressively.
- Dish stays centered and mostly static — these are not high-motion food-tossing reels, they're
  closer to slow, atmospheric product reveals (e.g. the Naan reel, the 2026 New Year reel).
- Music is often soft/ambient ("Simple Piano Melody," "Original audio," or the venue's own audio)
  — calm, not upbeat or fast-cut, which refines the earlier "increase speed" feedback: speed
  complaints were likely about specific videos feeling sluggish/unclear, not a standing request for
  fast-paced editing as the house style. The reliable default for new reels is calm/atmospheric
  pacing with a clean, legible build-up, not high-energy cuts.
- Seasonal reels (2026 New Year) reuse the standard logo header + bilingual headline + dish photo
  formula, just animated, with festive elements (fireworks, ornaments) layered behind.

**Content calendar is more varied than the rejection-history alone suggested.** Confirmed post
types beyond single-dish hero posts:
- Monthly menu update announcements (e.g. "4月份更新菜單公告") with a wide spread of dishes laid out on
  one table/banana leaf, shot like a feast.
- Combo/value-meal pricing graphics with clearly boxed price tags (e.g. "MEAL 1...200 NTD") — dense
  but organized, price always paired with both Chinese and English dish name.
- Delivery platform promos (Uber Eats) with the platform's own green logo treatment integrated.
- Festive/seasonal greetings (Mother's Day, New Year) blending the brand template with seasonal
  motifs (peonies, fireworks, gift boxes).
- Candid, unposed photos of real customers/staff at the restaurant (birthday celebration) — these
  appear without the heavy template treatment, suggesting authentic candid moments are posted more
  simply, without forcing the full branded header treatment onto every single image in a carousel.
- **Action**: Not every asset needs the full branded template treatment — candid/behind-the-scenes
  content can be simpler. Reserve the full header+typography treatment for planned promotional posts
  (menu items, offers, campaigns).

**Engagement scale context**: likes on posts range roughly 3–27. This is a small local business
account, not an influencer page — the bar for "successful" content is realistic, polished local
marketing, not viral-style production value. Don't over-engineer for reach; consistency and brand
correctness matter more than chasing high engagement numbers.

## 3. What tends to go smoothly (low-friction patterns)

- "Own ideas" briefs (client gives no specific requirement, e.g. some Tuesday/Friday posts) were
  often approved in 1 round — suggesting that once the general house style is internalized, low-spec
  briefs are actually lower-risk than tightly-specified ones, *provided* the established preferences
  (bilingual text, real photography, contrast, Tamil-Taiwan blend where relevant) are still applied
  by default.
- Repeat-format content (weekly Tuesday/Friday posts) gets faster approval over time as the format
  is established — front-load extra care into the *first* instance of a new recurring format.
