# Product Description Generation Guide — New Shop Trichy

Used whenever a post spotlights a specific product (not a festival/theme post). Every product post gets an **auto-generated description** — Vengatesh shouldn't have to write one from scratch each time.

## What to auto-generate

For any product named or shown, produce:

1. **Product name** — exact, as it appears in `product_categories.md` / the source list
2. **Category tag** — short label (e.g. "Chocolate", "Energy Drink", "Ice Cream") from `product_categories.md`
3. **A 2–3 sentence description**, written to be appetizing/attractive, following the rules below
4. **A short CTA line, written specifically for this product/offer** — don't default to "Grab yours today" or any other fixed phrase on every post. Pick an angle that fits the product: product-based (e.g. "Taste the Crunch", "Unwrap the Joy"), offer-based (e.g. "Save While It Lasts"), or urgency-based (e.g. "In Stock Now"). Check `posted_content_registry.md`'s layout/pattern log and avoid repeating the last post's CTA.

## Rules for the description text

- **Sensory and benefit-driven language is fine and encouraged**: texture, taste, mood, occasion ("rich, creamy", "perfect with your evening chai", "a quick energy boost before your commute"). This is standard, safe ad copywriting.
- **Never invent specific factual claims** not confirmed in `product_categories.md` or told directly by Vengatesh: no nutrition numbers, no "sugar-free"/"low-calorie"/certification claims, no ingredient lists, no comparative claims ("India's #1", "doctor recommended") unless Vengatesh confirms them. Generic sensory description ("smooth, creamy chocolate") is not a factual claim; a specific one ("30% less sugar") is — that line needs a source.
- **Don't fabricate brand taglines or slogans** as if official — write original descriptive copy instead. (Real official taglines can be used if Vengatesh confirms them.)
- **Tie to the outlet's context where it fits naturally** — commuters grabbing something before a train, a quick pick-me-up near the hospital, a treat on the way home — but don't force it if it doesn't fit the product.
- **Keep it short.** 2–3 sentences for the description, one line for the CTA. This is a social post, not a product page.

## Example templates by category

**Chocolate/confectionery:**
> "[Product name] — [category, e.g. rich milk chocolate with fruit & nut]. Smooth, indulgent, and perfect for sharing (or not). Grab a bar on your way through."

**Chips/savory snacks:**
> "[Product name] brings the crunch. [Flavor note if known, e.g. bold, spicy, tangy]. The perfect grab-and-go snack for your platform wait."

**Energy drinks:**
> "[Product name] — a quick lift when you need it. Cold, ready, and just steps from the platform."

**Ice cream:**
> "[Product name] — creamy, cool, and exactly what a Trichy afternoon calls for."

**Juices/fruit drinks:**
> "[Product name] — real fruit refreshment, chilled and ready. A better break than a queue at the station stall."

**Protein/health bars:**
> "[Product name] — a quick, wholesome bite that keeps you going, whether you're catching a train or a shift."

Adapt tone per product; these are starting patterns, not fill-in-the-blank templates to use verbatim every time — vary the wording so posts don't all read identically.

## Design placement

The description block needs its own visual space in the layout — not just a one-line tagline. Default pattern (proven, use this unless a simpler post is specifically requested):

- **Hook headline first, product name second** — lead with an emotional/benefit-driven line (e.g. "Your chocolate craving, solved."), not the product name. People stop scrolling for a feeling, not a SKU.
- **"NOW IN STOCK" or similar urgency ribbon** — small diagonal badge, creates freshness/urgency
- **Radial glow behind the hero product shot** — draws the eye instead of the product sitting flat on black
- **Real CTA button, not plain text** — styled as a button (rounded rect, filled yellow, bold black text), with wording that's product/offer-specific per the CTA rule above, not a reused fixed phrase
- **Short, punchy description fragments** over full sentences — "Rich. Creamy. Loaded with Real Fruit & Nut." hits faster than a complete sentence when someone's scrolling
- Still follows the mandatory background rule (see `brand_guidelines.md`) — strictly black + yellow, no exceptions, no photoreal scenery.

## Benefit icon list — proven pattern, use for product posts

A row of 3 short benefit points, stacked vertically, each with:
- A small circular icon (outline style, yellow)
- A bold yellow label (2–3 words, e.g. "RICH & CREAMY")
- One short white subtext line under it (e.g. "Smoothest Dairy Milk Silk")

Pick 3 genuinely true benefits per product — sensory/experiential ones are safe (rich & creamy, real fruit & nut, perfect for breaks), don't invent factual/nutritional claims (see rules above). This sits alongside the hook headline, not replacing the 2–3 sentence description — use whichever fits the layout better, or both if there's room.
