## Hard brand rules (never break these)

1. **Logo always at the top, always readable.** The lockup is: "RAJA RANI" in a bold gold/champagne serif-display font (largest), "INDIAN RESTAURANT" underneath in a smaller gold or white tracked-out caps font, and the tagline "Taste of Chettinad" beneath that in a thin gold script or italic serif. It sits centered at the very top of the poster on the black background, sometimes with a subtle gold rule or laurel/ornament flourish around it. There is no separate transparent logo file in the Drive folder — recreate this lockup faithfully (same three-line structure, same relative sizes) rather than inventing a new logo treatment.
   - **Crown color variant (confirmed across multiple approved posters):** the crown icon above "RAJA RANI" sometimes renders solid black (outline/silhouette style) instead of the usual gold-gradient crown, typically on posters with a bright yellow/gold (rather than black) background, or on bold high-contrast product shots (e.g. Chilli Paneer, Karampodi Shrimp, Paneer Tikka spotlights). Both the gold-gradient crown and the solid-black crown are approved — pick whichever has better contrast against that poster's background rather than defaulting to only one.
2. **Black-and-yellow (gold) premium theme, always.** Background is black (or a very dark charcoal/black gradient) in essentially every poster. Gold/yellow (`#D4A017`–`#FFC72C` range, warm metallic gold rather than flat lemon yellow) carries headlines, prices, dividers, and the logo. White or cream is the secondary text color for body copy/menu items. Occasional small red/green accents appear only inside food photography itself (never as a UI color) or in the halal badge. Even festival posters (Diwali, Tamil New Year, Valentine's Day) keep the black-and-gold base and layer the festival motif (diyas, banana leaf, hearts) on top rather than replacing the palette.
   - **Dark-brown/bronze exception (confirmed — Kulfi poster):** dessert/mithai-style spotlights may use a warm dark-brown or bronze backdrop instead of pure black, to evoke an antique brass/sweet-shop setting. This is a narrow, deliberate exception for sweets/desserts only — don't generalize it to other poster types.
3. **Footer must always include, every poster, no exceptions:**
   - Phone: `+1 734-404-5523`
   - Address: `45172 Ford Road Canton, MI 48187`
   - Website: `www.rajaranimichigan.com`
   - Social: `raja_rani_indian_restaurant` (works as both IG and FB reference; sometimes both an Instagram glyph and Facebook glyph are shown next to the same handle)
   - These are usually stacked in a slim footer band, small caps or small serif, still gold/white on black.
4. **Halal badge.** Include the Halal certification badge/roundel (Arabic "حلال" + "HALAL FOOD 100%") somewhere in the lower-third of the poster unless the client explicitly says to omit it for a specific design (e.g. pure greeting/press-mention posters occasionally drop it).
5. **"ORDER NOW" or event-appropriate CTA.** Food/menu posters almost always carry a gold pill/badge CTA — "ORDER NOW" is the default. For event posters (Banana Leaf Feast, buffet dates), swap in the matching action: "REGISTER NOW," "BOOK YOUR SPOT," a date range, etc. — the CTA style (gold rounded badge, bold caps) stays the same even when the words change.
6. **Real, appetizing food photography.** Every dish-focused poster uses a styled, high-contrast hero photo of the actual food (steam, glossy sauce, garnish) — never a flat stock icon or an AI-generated/illustrated look passed off as real. If no client-uploaded photo exists for a requested dish, check the official site (`rajaranimichigan.com`) for real dish photos/menu listings first, then search the web for a genuine food-photography image of that dish (matching the glossy, high-contrast styling of past Raja Rani posters) if the site doesn't have it — real sourced photos are strongly preferred over AI-generated placeholders. Only fall back to a clearly-labeled AI-generated placeholder if no suitable real photo can be found, and say so explicitly rather than silently substituting one.

## Soft / stylistic patterns (follow unless the brief says otherwise)

- **Weekend Special posters** (the most frequent weekly job) stack 4–9 dish names with prices in a clean vertical list or two-column Veg/Non-Veg split, gold for dish names or prices, white for secondary text, one to three food photos framed with a thin gold border or soft glow, gold "WEEKEND SPECIAL" headline directly under the logo.
- **Single hero dish** posters keep copy minimal: a 3–6 word evocative tagline ("Rich, rustic, and irresistibly spicy," "Crispy. Spicy. Addictive.") in a mix of a thin gold script/serif line plus one bold caps word, dish name as the visual focus, and the same footer/badge/CTA furniture.
- **Combo & bundle** posters (holiday biryani combos, spring/summer combo menus, snack/drink/main mini-menus) use a clean itemized price list/table, often with a "LIMITED-TIME" or dated banner.
- **Festival/seasonal** posters (Diwali, Tamil New Year & Ugadi Banana Leaf Feast, Valentine's Day, Women's Day, New Year) layer festival-specific motifs and colors (diyas, banana leaf spreads, hearts) onto the black-gold base, and often swap "Order Now" for an event CTA with a date/time.
- **Recurring weekly promos** (Chat Night on Wednesdays, Chinese Combo Night on Tuesdays, Unlimited Dosa Night) always state the specific day and time window prominently, plus "Dine In" restrictions when applicable.
- **Loyalty/trust posters** (stamp card, gift cards, press mentions, testimonials) are more typography-led than food-photo-led, but never abandon the black-gold palette or footer. Gift-card posters get reskinned seasonally (e.g. a Christmas/holiday red-and-garland version with struck-through discount pricing) while keeping the same card-mockup mechanic — seasonal reskinning is expected, not a deviation.
- **"Foodie Review" testimonials** are a distinct sub-format from press/award mentions: customer name + verified checkmark + 5-star row + a short quote naming specific dishes + 1-2 photos of those dishes, on a stamp-edged card. Use this format when the ask is "share a customer review," and the press/award format (badge + headline claim, no star rating) when the ask is "share a press mention or ranking."
- **Buy-one-get-one-free / free-add-on promos** ("Buy X, Get Y Free") are their own category, separate from priced combos: two stacked panels, alternating black-on-gold and gold-on-black bands, one food photo per panel, no price shown since the mechanic is a free add-on rather than a bundle price.
- Occasionally a hero-dish spotlight uses a lighter cream/gold background with a faint temple-gopuram silhouette watermark and a "PEOPLE'S CHOICE ★★★★★" ribbon badge instead of the usual black base — reserve this for an accolade/award framing, not as a general spotlight alternative. This same ribbon badge has also appeared on a bright-yellow background with bold black type — both are valid "People's Choice" treatments; choose refined (cream/temple) vs. punchy (yellow/black) based on tone.
- Small hand-drawn doodle accents (arrows, stars, motion-lines, a scrawled "MUST TRY") are an approved lightweight embellishment on hero-dish spotlights — use sparingly, not on every poster.
- Festival posters aren't always a discount: some run a raffle/prize-drawing mechanic instead (e.g. Women's Day silver-coin giveaway) — confirm with the client which mechanic (discount %, free item, or raffle) applies before designing.
- When a festival date has a natural number in it (e.g. February 14th), it's fine to echo that number in the offer (14% off) if it reads naturally as a fun coincidence — don't force this pairing when it doesn't fit.
- Recurring weekly-promo variety counts (e.g. "15 Varieties of Dosa" vs. "25+ Varieties") change between postings — always confirm the current count with the client/menu rather than reusing a number from an older reference poster.
- Gold dividing rules, small flourish ornaments, and rounded gold badges (for prices or CTAs) recur throughout — treat these as safe default embellishments for a new poster if the brief doesn't specify layout details.

## Menu sourcing rule
Always pull dish names and prices from `assets/menu.json` (raw) or `references/menu_reference.md` (condensed, human-readable) as the primary source of truth — never invent a dish name or price. The official site (`rajaranimichigan.com`) is also a legitimate live source for current menu listings and dish photos — cross-check there if a dish isn't in the local menu file or if prices may be stale. If a client request names a dish that isn't found in either the menu file or the site, flag that explicitly rather than guessing at a price.
